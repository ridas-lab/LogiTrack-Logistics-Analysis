This Week 4 report presents an executed Python workflow for predictive modeling and resource allocation in logistics. The project forecasts the number of days from order approval to customer delivery for a hypothetical ecommerce marketplace, using prepared Olist records as a historical benchmark. It also includes a separate experiment with 2,000 explicitly simulated shipments to document the hypothetical dataset, its features, and the target generation process.

The historical analysis compares constant baselines, Ridge regression, decision trees, and histogram gradient boosting. Chronological training, validation, and test cohorts are used, with an outcome maturity rule to reduce unequal follow-up. The report explains preprocessing, feature availability, hyperparameter choices, and safeguards against using future delivery information. Model performance is assessed using mean absolute error, root mean squared error, R squared, bias, and error percentiles.

The selected gradient boosting model achieves a test mean absolute error of 4.67 days and root mean squared error of 6.64 days on 19,654 eligible completed orders. Six embedded figures created with Matplotlib and Seaborn show model comparisons, prediction errors, monthly and geographical variation, feature importance, allocation results, and the simulated experiment. Each figure includes an interpretation and relevant limitations.

A worked optimization example allocates an assumed 100 reviews per active day. At equal capacity, model priority selects 428 known late deliveries compared with 355 for first in first out. These results measure retrospective identification, not prevented delays or proven cost savings. The report concludes with actionable recommendations for a controlled pilot, better operational snapshots, monitoring, and further treatment of extreme delays. Python code, prepared data, result tables, verification checks, and reproducibility instructions accompany the report.
