This Week 3 report presents an executed analysis of logistics performance using Python, with embedded visualizations, statistical summaries, interpretations, and recommendations. It continues the ecommerce delivery project by examining the order-level dataset prepared during Week 2. The historical benchmark contains 99,441 orders, with 96,470 eligible completed orders used for delivery-cycle and on-time performance calculations.

The exploratory analysis covers means, medians, dispersion, percentiles, distributions, monthly order volumes, destination-state performance, delivery stages, and relationships among freight charges, order values, product weights, product volumes, and delivery times. The measured on-time delivery rate is 93.23%, while the median delivery cycle is 10.22 days. The report explains the reason for selecting each visualization and connects its findings to operational decisions. It also identifies the importance of examining both late-order counts and late-delivery rates when prioritizing regions.

A separate hypothetical dataset of 2,000 shipments demonstrates transportation-cost analysis and sensitivity testing. Its generation rules and assumed cost formula are documented, and simulated outcomes are clearly distinguished from observed historical results. Recorded freight charges are treated as customer charges rather than actual operating costs.

The submission includes Python code excerpts, reproducible scripts, chart images, supporting result tables, data provenance, and explicit analysis denominators. It discusses missing information, invalid milestones, extreme observations, and the limits of correlation. Recommendations focus on investigating transit delays, improving milestone data, and testing targeted interventions before claiming savings or service improvements. The report therefore combines practical analysis with a transparent explanation of what the evidence can and cannot establish.
